Your national page

This information appears on your country’s page on the Lausanne Europe website. Please write it in your national language. If your page is in more than one language, fill in one form for each language group.

Every movement is at a different stage, so don’t worry if you can’t fill in everything: share what you have. We’ll ask you to review it once a year.

1 · Your movement

As it should appear on the page, e.g. “Movimiento de Lausana España”.

2 · Communications coordinator

The communications coordinator looks after your page and receives messages sent through it. It can be your national leader, but doesn’t have to be: anyone in your movement who has time to handle communications is welcome.

Not shown on the website. Messages sent from the “Contact coordinator” button reach you here.

3 · Page content (in your national language)

Please write this section in your national language, so your page speaks directly to people in your country. The examples are in English only to show the idea.

When and how the movement started, who is involved, your vision, and what you are doing now.

See an example (in English)

Lausanne Genovia sprang into life after the Fourth Lausanne Congress in 2024, with a particular vision to help Genovia’s historic churches collaborate in mission with the new diaspora churches.

The movement has been holding a series of regional Lausanne gatherings in cities around the country and has launched a podcast on whole-life discipleship. Its first Lausanne Genovia National Gathering is planned for June 2027.

Religious background, how the evangelical church developed, and what is changing today.

See an example (in English)

Genovia is primarily Catholic, with Lutheran churches since the 1700s. The evangelical movement began with a revival in 1926 and grew with the arrival of Baptist, Pentecostal and independent missionaries in the 1960s. Since 2004, migration from Latin America has brought significant growth in diaspora churches across the country.
